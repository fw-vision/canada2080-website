## The scenario

By 2080, every household has sovereign compute for its health records, financial data, and other sensitive processes. Each household controls the systems that manage its most personal information, rather than surrendering every essential function to a distant platform.

Households do not operate alone. They join with neighbours, buildings, local institutions, and regional infrastructure to form resilient compute clusters. The cluster can share capacity for non-sensitive work while keeping sensitive processes under local control.

## Resilience starts with the part

A resilient national system begins with resilient parts. A household that can manage essential processes, retain control of sensitive data, and continue operating through disruption contributes more than individual security. It becomes a capable unit within a wider community.

Connected capable units can share surplus capacity, provide redundancy, and reduce dependence on a single failure point. Communities become more resilient when their members can both receive support and contribute capacity. Regions can then connect these communities through standards, trusted exchange, and infrastructure that strengthens rather than replaces local capability.

## Why AI changes the scale

Complex systems once demanded expensive controls, specialist operators, and infrastructure large enough to justify them. That made tailored management economical mainly at large institutional scale.

AI and affordable compute change the equation. They can help households, farms, buildings, and small communities manage complexity that previously required centralized systems: energy use, water, food production, health information, financial administration, and local data.

This does not eliminate the need for public infrastructure, professional expertise, strong privacy safeguards, or trusted institutions. It changes where capability can begin. Resilience becomes practical from the household upward: capable units, connected communities, strong regions, and a sovereign nation.

## The test

Canada2080 treats this as a scenario to guide design and experimentation. It should be tested through measures including service continuity, recovery time, affordability, privacy, cyber resilience, accessibility, energy use, and distributional impact.
