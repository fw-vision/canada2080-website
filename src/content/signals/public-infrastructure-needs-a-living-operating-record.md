Infrastructure proposals describe expected performance. Communities experience actual performance through grid demand, water systems, noise, emissions, employment, service reliability, and the response when something fails.

**A living operating record** connects those two views. It follows a facility from approval through construction, operation, maintenance, disruption, renewal, and closure. The record gives communities, operators, institutions, and policy leaders a common basis for judging whether commitments are being met.

## Approval begins the record

Digital infrastructure changes after a planning decision. Equipment is replaced, workloads grow, cooling systems respond to seasonal conditions, grid constraints shift, ownership changes, and new cyber threats emerge. A one-time project description cannot represent that full operating life.

The public record should name the operating owner, define each measure, state the reporting interval, and preserve a route for questions and complaints. Material changes should update the record before old assumptions become the basis for new impacts.

## Report conditions and outcomes

A useful scorecard separates nameplate capacity from measured operation. It should report comparable information across five areas:

- **Energy and resilience**: contracted and measured IT load, total facility load, annual electricity use, peak demand, connection status, demand response, outages, recovery time, and maintenance.
- **Water, heat, noise, and backup**: cooling design, water withdrawal and consumption where applicable, heat-use performance, generator tests, fuel use, noise readings, emissions compliance, and complaint resolution.
- **Cyber and data governance**: accountable roles, audit scope, incident-disclosure rules, data classification, residency and flow maps, key-management status, and tested exit paths.
- **Workforce and procurement**: operating roles, training, local and Canadian procurement, supplier development, and the capacity to repair and improve the system.
- **Community and rights commitments**: benefit-sharing, affordability effects, emergency procedures, rights-holder engagement where applicable, and performance against stated public outcomes.

Technical standards can strengthen the record. NIST's Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 and ISO/IEC 27001 provide structures for cyber risk and information-security management. Environmental reporting programmes provide another reference point for defined measures and recurring disclosure. Each tool works best when readers can see its scope, assumptions, and relationship to actual operations.

## Capital support should buy capability

On September 15, 2026, the federal government announced the Productivity Mega Deduction as part of a wider effort to encourage capital investment. The announcement increases the importance of distinguishing investment activity from durable capability.

Public incentives, planning decisions, grid upgrades, and community trust should produce more than installed assets. They should strengthen reliable service, skilled operation, supplier capacity, accountable data practices, resilience, and reinvestment. A living operating record makes those outcomes visible across the life of the infrastructure.

## Mission relevance

This signal connects three Canada2080 missions:

- **Sovereign energy and compute** links reliable low-carbon power with Canadian-accessible digital capacity and cyber resilience.
- **Climate-ready regions and managed growth** requires infrastructure that fits local energy, water, service, affordability, and adaptation conditions.
- **Human capability and institutional learning** depends on skilled operators, public measurement, procurement learning, and institutions that improve through evidence.

Canada will earn infrastructure legitimacy through performance that remains inspectable after the announcement, the approval, and the opening day.
